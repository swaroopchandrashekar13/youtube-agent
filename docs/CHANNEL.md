# Launch language and niche: recommendation

Status: **recommendation, awaiting owner confirmation.** Researched 2026-11-13. Most market numbers below come from vendor blogs with no published method. Treat them as directional only, and re-check against YouTube Studio data once the channel exists.

## Recommendation

| | |
|---|---|
| Language | **Kannada (`kn`), code-mixed register (`script.style = codemix`)**: Kannada sentences that keep English technical terms (API, resume, interview, SQL) as spoken in Bengaluru IT |
| Niche | **Tech careers for Kannada speakers**: getting the first IT job and growing in it (interview preparation, resumes, skills roadmaps, how hiring works, salary and offer basics *without* financial advice) |
| Audience | Karnataka students and early-career engineers (0-5 years), including Tier-2/3 towns, who learn faster in Kannada |
| Format | 6-10 minute explainers, one idea per video. Shorts later, once long-form quality is proven |
| Fallback | If the Phase 4 blind bake-off scores Kannada narration below about **3.5/5 average naturalness**, launch in **English (`en-IN`) with the same niche** and add Kannada later as a second channel (the design already supports this) |

## Why

1. **Competition is thin where it matters.** Kannada *tech* channels exist and are large (gadget and app tips: Kannada Tech For You ~1.9M, TECH In Kannada ~1.2M subscribers per third-party trackers). But I found **no clearly identified Kannada-language channel focused on IT interview preparation and early careers**; the closest are English or bilingual training channels. English tech-career content, by contrast, is one of the most saturated niches on YouTube.
2. **Demand is local and durable.** Bengaluru is India's largest IT hiring market. Fresher jobs and interview prep are evergreen searches, and regional-language audiences are described as unusually loyal.
3. **It fits the content rules.** Careers is not finance, medical, or legal advice (rule 7), and claims are easy to source (official job posts, company career pages, government skill programmes).
4. **It fits the pipeline you specified.** The prompt already plans code-mixed passages ("API, resume, interview"), Kannada fonts, and a Kannada TTS bake-off. A career niche makes the most of that work.
5. **It suits YouTube's 2026 "inauthentic content" enforcement.** Monetisation now targets template-like, mass-produced channels at the *channel* level. A narrow niche with original analysis per video (own examples, own visuals, real job-market evidence) is the safe side of that line. The sameness check in Phase 7 enforces it.

## Trade-offs to accept

- **Lower RPM than English.** Sources claim English earns roughly 2-8× more per view than regional languages, and Kannada education/career CPM estimates vary widely (₹25-130 per 1,000 depending on source). Lower competition and loyalty are the bet; revenue per view will be lower.
- **Kannada TTS quality is the biggest risk.** Sarvam Bulbul v3 lists `kn-IN` and claims strong code-mixing, but there's no independent Kannada benchmark. One practitioner found Kannada in Latin script misread, and pure formal Kannada sounding "newsreader-like". This is exactly why the blind bake-off in Phase 4 must decide, not vendor claims.
- **You (or a Kannada-speaking reviewer) must review every script and preview.** Approval is already mandatory; for Kannada it also has to cover pronunciation and register.

## Decision needed

1. Confirm **Kannada (code-mixed) + tech careers**, or choose English or another niche.
2. Name a **channel display name** (needed in Phase 5 briefs and Phase 8 metadata), or ask for options.
